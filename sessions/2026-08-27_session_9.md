# Session 9: a pack does not know your vocabulary

**Date**: 2026-08-27
**Branch**: `feature/session-9-slotmap` (worktree, `.claude/worktrees/session-9-slotmap`)

## Why now

Session 8 shipped `conform` and the consuming project tried to use it on the
first real asset it had. It refused, correctly, and the refusal exposed a gap
in the specification rather than in the code.

Conform was specified as walking material slots *named after swatches*. That
is true of a mesh somebody authored for this project, because they chose the
names. The same document lists a CC0 tavern pack as needing re-pinning through
this verb, and that pack names its materials `Wood` and `Metal`, because it was
made by somebody who had never heard of this project's atlas. Both cases were
in scope from the start and only one of them had a mechanism.

## What the consuming project actually hit

It reached for a prop from the pack it already vendors, and conform refused
with one slot, named `texture`, naming no swatch. Reading the mesh explains
both halves: that prop has a single material slot and 216 distinct uvs
spanning two atlas cells. It is already atlas-native, and conforming it would
have collapsed the whole prop onto one texel and destroyed it.

So the refusal was right twice over, and worth writing down: **conform is not
for a mesh already on the atlas.** Nothing in the verb says that, and nothing
needs to, because a prop whose slots are not swatch names is refused anyway.
The gate that exists to catch a half-conformed mesh catches this too.

## The mapping

`--map SLOT=SWATCH`, repeatable, matched case-insensitively on the slot
because a pack that writes `Wood` and a person who types `wood` mean the same
thing. It resolves ahead of the slot's own name, so an authored mesh keeps
working untouched and a bought one becomes expressible.

A flag rather than a manifest key. The mapping is a fact about one pack rather
than about the project, and a project may take props from several; putting it
in the manifest would mean one table pretending to be about all of them.

The slot gate now names the flag and the slot it wants, since a message that
says only what is wrong makes the reader go and find the manual.

## Verification

- 372 tests, up from 365.
- A mesh authored to look bought, with slots `Wood` and `Metal` and uvs from a
  foreign layout, refused without a map and named both slots and the flag.
- The same mesh with `--map Wood=wood_mid --map Metal=iron`, conformed against
  the consuming project's real atlas: 12 triangles, one TEXCOORD set, one image
  uri, and exactly two distinct uvs, being the two texels the table names.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **A specification can be internally inconsistent and read as complete.** The
  same page asked for slots named after swatches and named a bought pack as
  input. Neither sentence is wrong; together they need a mechanism that was
  not there, and only contact with a real asset showed it.
- **Conform is for a mesh that is not on the atlas yet.** One already on it has
  real per-face uvs across many cells, and re-pinning would flatten it. The
  slot gate refuses that case as a side effect of refusing unnamed slots.

## What is next

- The validator remains wired and unproven, as at the end of session 8.
- Persisting a mapping per pack, on the record rather than in the manifest, if
  re-conforming the same pack ever becomes routine. Not built, because one
  flag repeated is fine for one pack and speculating past that would be
  designing for a use nobody has had yet.
