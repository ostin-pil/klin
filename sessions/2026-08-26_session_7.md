# Session 7 — Checking the licence before the GPU starts

**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Branch:** `feature/session-7-gen` (worktree `.claude/worktrees/session-7-gen`)

The third verb in the ratified table. Session 6 built the index and, in doing
so, made this one worth building: a workflow names its models, the index
resolves names to ledger records, and the policy engine judges records. The
chain was complete before `gen` existed, so the only thing missing was
something to run it at the right moment.

## Why this is not a script

Driving a sampler takes eighty lines and the consuming project already had
them. What a script cannot do is answer the question that matters before the
work happens rather than after it.

```
workflow design/mockups/workflows/flux_dev_style_t2i.json
  checkpoint flux1-dev-fp8.safetensors    LicenseRef-FLUX-1-dev-Non-Commercial [noncommercial]

output could ship: NO
  excluded-licences: licence LicenseRef-FLUX-1-dev-Non-Commercial is noncommercial
```

`--check` exits non-zero on that and queues nothing. Without it klin says so and
generates anyway, because a prototype plate from a non-commercial model is a
legitimate thing to want.

This inverts the usual order. The alternative is what Barinn actually did:
generate 1,384 plates over several weeks, then discover that 66 came from
FLUX.1-dev and cannot ship. The models were identical either way. Only the
moment of finding out changed, and that is the whole product here.

## Two boundaries held

**Raw output does not enter the ledger.** A record per generated image would put
thousands of rows describing throwaway plates into a file meant to describe
shipped assets. Outputs go to the index, which is derived and disposable and
sits outside every checkout; a ledger record appears when an image becomes an
asset, and that is `conform`, which does not exist yet.

**Outputs are indexed as they land**, so a run cannot accumulate into an
unattributed pile. That was the state session 6 found: 1,384 images, none of
them indexed by anything.

## Roles by traversal, and the two defects that found

The driver this was ported from located the positive prompt by looking for a
`CLIPTextEncode` whose title contained "POSITIVE". That works until somebody
renames a node in the editor, and then it fails on a template that is perfectly
valid. `klin.index.comfy` already locates every role by walking the graph from
the sampler, because it had to read graphs nobody wrote for it, and the same
walk works for writing. So a template needs no special titles, and the reader
and the writer cannot disagree about which node is which.

Patching all six of Barinn's real templates and reading back where each value
landed found two defects that no synthetic graph in the test file reached.

**A direction-blind walk follows the wrong edge.** `ControlNetApplyAdvanced`
takes a positive *and* a negative conditioning. A walk that always tried
`positive` first went down the positive branch for both, and resolved both
prompts to one node. The walk now prefers the key matching the branch it came
down.

**Flux and Z-Image have no separate negative prompt.** Their templates derive it
from the positive through a `ConditioningZeroOut`, so the graph holds one
encoder. Writing `--negative` there overwrote the positive prompt, and the
combination of the two defects is what surfaced it: the probe reported
`pos=MISS neg=SET` on two real templates, which is the signature of one node
being written twice. Left alone it would have produced a plate whose record said
one prompt and whose pixels came from another, which is the exact failure the
ledger exists to prevent. It is refused by name now.

Both are now regression tests built from the shapes the real templates use.

## Verification

The pre-flight check runs against Barinn's real workflows and its real ledger.
`flux_dev_style_t2i` reports NO on FLUX.1-dev's non-commercial licence and exits
1 under `--check`; the schnell and Z-Image templates report yes on Apache-2.0.
Patching is checked against all six templates by reading back where every value
landed.

The whole loop then ran for real against a live ComfyUI, which is the part a
stub cannot prove:

```
klin gen comfy --workflow zimage_turbo_t2i.json --seed 424242 --size 640x368 \
    --prompt "the interior of a railway bar car ..."

  unet   z_image_turbo_bf16.safetensors    apache-2.0 [permissive]
output could ship: yes
queueing seed 424242 (1/1)
  D:\ComfyUI\output\klin_gen_probe_00001_.png
indexed into ...
```

Twelve seconds, and `klin show klin_gen_probe` then reports the image back with
its seed, sampler, prompt and a base model resolved to
`hf-Comfy-Org--z_image_turbo` by filesystem identity, verdict `ship: yes`. The
generate, index, trace and judge path is closed end to end, which it was not
before this session: an image made now is traceable at the moment it exists
rather than by a scan months later.

The probe image is left in the output tree deliberately, as the evidence.
